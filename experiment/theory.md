# Theory

## Introduction

Diffusion-Limited Aggregation (DLA) is a physical process in which randomly moving particles stick together when they come into contact with an existing cluster. Over time, these particles form branching and irregular structures known as fractals.

## Fractal Dimension

Fractal dimension describes how a pattern fills space and measures its geometric complexity. Unlike simple geometric shapes, fractals may have non-integer dimensions.

## Box-Counting Method

The box-counting method is used to estimate the fractal dimension of a cluster. The pattern is covered with boxes of different sizes, and the number of boxes containing part of the cluster is counted.

The fractal dimension is estimated using:

D = Δlog N(ε) / Δlog(1/ε)

Where:
- D = Fractal dimension
- N(ε) = Number of boxes containing the cluster
- ε = Size of each box

For a two-dimensional DLA cluster, the estimated fractal dimension is generally around 1.7 to 1.8, depending on the simulation and measurement method.
